Hamlet Basics

A quick guide to Shakespeare's famous tragedy

The Core Story

Hamlet, Prince of Denmark, discovers from his father's ghost that his uncle, Claudius, murdered the king to seize the throne and marry Hamlet's mother, Gertrude. The ghost demands revenge. Hamlet, torn between grief, rage, and philosophical doubt, feigns madness while grappling with the morality of his task, leading to a tragic chain of events.

Key Characters

Hamlet
The melancholy, philosophical prince. He is introspective, witty, and paralyzed by indecision.
Claudius
The new King. Charismatic, politically shrewd, and morally corrupt.
Gertrude
Hamlet's mother. She married Claudius shortly after her husband's death.
Ghost
The spirit of Hamlet's murdered father, demanding revenge.
Ophelia
Hamlet's love interest. Her descent into madness mirrors Hamlet's feigned insanity.
Polonius
Ophelia's father, the lord chamberlain. Long-winded, nosy, and manipulative.
Horatio
Hamlet's loyal friend. The voice of reason and the play's primary witness.
Laertes
Ophelia's brother. Hot-headed and impulsive, serving as a foil to Hamlet.

Major Themes

  • Revenge vs. Justice: Is Hamlet's quest morally right?
  • Madness: Real vs. feigned insanity and its causes.
  • Mortality: The famous "To be or not to be" soliloquy questions life, death, and the afterlife.
  • Betrayal & Corruption: Political and familial treachery poison the state of Denmark.
  • Action vs. Inaction: Hamlet's paralyzing over-analysis versus decisive action.
  • Appearance vs. Reality: "Something is rotten in the state of Denmark." Nothing is as it seems.

Famous Quotes

- Hamlet (Act 3, Scene 1)

- Marcellus (Act 1, Scene 4)

"The lady doth protest too much, methinks."
- Gertrude (Act 3, Scene 2)

Why It Matters

Hamlet is arguably the most famous play in the English language. Its exploration of the human psyche, moral ambiguity, and existential dread feels strikingly modern. The character of Hamlet himself—with his introspection, wit, and paralyzing self-doubt—has become the archetype of the modern individual.

Fun Fact: With over 4,000 lines, Hamlet has the most lines of any character in Shakespeare's plays.
